The Problem (1): Companies & Organisations

Core Problem (Business Side)

Companies and large NGOs need to decide where and how to invest (CSR, ESG, new services, products) across cities, but:

  • City-level data is fragmented, lagging, and spreadsheet-based
  • ESG and "impact" reporting is often high-level and not city-specific
  • They don't know which cities or neighbourhoods are actually struggling with which issues (affordability vs safety vs health vs mental health, etc.)

Pain Points for Companies / Orgs

No Fine-Grained Demand Signal

Hard to see where wellbeing issues are spiking right now (e.g. housing stress in a specific city).

Expensive Bespoke Research

Traditional market/social research is slow, manual, and costly for each geography.

Hard to Evaluate Impact: After launching a programme in a city, it's hard to measure whether local civic conditions are improving.

Concrete Examples

  • A healthcare company wants to expand mental health services but lacks city-level signals on stress, anxiety, and access to care.
  • A bank wants to target financial inclusion programmes but doesn't know which cities have the worst affordability and opportunity scores.
Slide 3

The Problem (2): Government, NGOs & Society

Core Problem (Public / NGO Side)

Governments, NGOs and social innovators need early signals on poverty and social exclusion, but:

  • Official data is slow (annual reports) and often coarse (city or territory level only)
  • Community voices show up in news and social media, but are not systematically analysed
  • There is no single, transparent civic health "dashboard" accessible to the public

Pain Points for Government / NGOs / Communities

Blind Spots

Issues (e.g. transport exclusion, unsafe housing, mental health) appear in local news and online discussions long before they show up in official statistics.

Fragmented Information

NGOs and agencies each see their own data but lack a shared, cross-city picture.

Hard for the Public to Understand Tradeoffs: Citizens can't easily see, for each city, what's working, what's failing, and why.

Hong Kong Angle

Hong Kong wants to target poverty and exclusion more precisely (e.g. by district or by issue), but needs continuous, low-cost intelligence drawn from open sources – in a fair, transparent, auditable way.

Two Angles, One Platform

Overall Solution

Our City Health: an AI-powered platform that:

  • Scores cities on 12 civic dimensions
  • Surfaces top issues and citations
  • Visualises results on a 3D globe and dashboard

How It Works

End-to-End Pipeline
Step 1: Data Collection

Scrape news sites and Reddit/social channels for each city. Use RSS/sitemaps + smart fallbacks to maximise coverage.

Step 2: City Tagging & Filtering

NLP + rules to tag which city each article/post is about. Filter out irrelevant content; deduplicate.

Step 3: Document Scoring & Fairness

Each article/post scored on: relevance (45%), source quality (30%), length (15%), recency (10%). Enforce per-city document caps so big, noisy cities don't dominate.

Step 4: LLM Analysis

Send balanced snippets to an LLM (e.g. GPT-4) with strict JSON schema. Get back overall city score, 12 dimension scores, rationales, top issues.

Step 5: Visualisation

Write results to JSON and render in: map.html (3D globe with clickable cities) and city_health_dashboard_MASSIVE.html (detailed city dashboard).

Innovation & Technology

Core Innovations

Hybrid Pipeline

Robust scraping + NLP + LLM scoring. Fairness built-in (document caps, diversity across sources).

Transparent AI

Every score tied back to underlying citations (articles/posts). Rationale text for each dimension, not just a number.

Interactive Visualisation: 3D globe with precise camera control and clickable markers. Smart label decluttering and zoom-sensitive behaviour.

Why This Is Different From Existing Indices

Most Urban Indices

  • Use static surveys / lagging indicators
  • Don't expose textual evidence or per-dimension rationales
  • Don't update as frequently and are not city-specific enough

Our City Health

  • Can be updated regularly (e.g. monthly/quarterly)
  • Offers issue-level granularity and multi-source evidence
  • Designed from day one for fairness and transparency
